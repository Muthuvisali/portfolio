Scope note

This is an independent portfolio study based on public information as of September 2026. It is not affiliated with Google and does not claim knowledge of Google's proprietary ranking, auction, or internal roadmap.

Executive Summary

The Thesis
in One Page

Observation

AI Search is shifting user behavior from discrete queries toward longer, multi turn sessions. Google says AI Mode has passed 1 billion monthly active users, its average query is about three times as long as a traditional Search query, and AI powered features are helping drive Search usage to all time highs.[2]

Business signal

Alphabet's Q2 2026 filing shows a sharp contrast. Google Search & other revenue rose from $54.2B to $63.3B year over year, while Google Network revenue slipped from $7.354B to $7.303B. Network impressions fell 12% while cost per impression rose 13%, and Alphabet cited a revenue mix shift from Network properties toward Search & other properties.[1]

Current direction

Google is already integrating ads into AI Search. Ads can appear within AI Overviews, matched against both the original query and AI generated content. In AI Mode, Google is testing Conversational Discovery ads, Highlighted Answers, AI powered Shopping ads, and expanded Direct Offers.[3][4]

Gap investigated

Those public products validate answer aware ad matching. This case explores a more granular layer: what if a structured AI answer is decomposed into distinct micro intents, each independently scored for commercial eligibility, while only a small number of high utility sponsored recommendations are shown?

Product hypothesis

If conversational Search can identify commercial micro intents and selectively match clearly labeled sponsored recommendations to them, it may create higher context commercial interactions than generic query level matching, without turning the assistant into an ad catalog.

Core question

Can deeper conversational context create fewer, more useful, more valuable sponsored interactions without reducing trust, answer quality, or organic discovery?

Problem Definition

The Unit of Behavior
Is Changing

Traditional Search monetization is naturally organized around discrete queries. AI Search increasingly behaves like a session: the user starts broad, refines constraints, compares options, and may complete a decision without leaving the conversation.

Query centric model
Query → intent → ad eligibility → ranking / auction
→
Conversational model
Conversation → answer structure → micro intents → selected commercial moments

The starting observation was personal but testable: in AI Mode, a user can often complete discovery inside the answer and follow up box rather than opening multiple source pages.

Experience Design

Two Patterns: Sequential
and Categorical Micro Intents

A. Sequential: Korean skincare routine

A single request can produce a structured routine. The system scores each step but does not turn every step into an ad slot. A 7 to 10 step response may still contain only one to three sponsored units.

The table below is the prototype's actual decision trace for "Build me a Korean skincare routine for dry skin under $120". Every step clears the eligibility bar, so the choice comes down to placement priority (commercial intent × ad quality), the per-answer cap, and a rule that no advertiser holds two slots in the same answer.

Answer componentCommercial scoreEligible?Placement decision
Sunscreen.91YesSponsored highest placement priority
Oil cleanser.88YesSponsored second slot
Serum.84YesNo saturation cap reached
Moisturizer.82YesNo diversity rule: same advertiser as the oil cleanser
Water cleanser.79YesNo saturation cap reached
Essence.71YesNo diversity rule: same advertiser as the sunscreen
Toner.67YesNo saturation; optional step, lowest intent

Being eligible is not the same as being shown. The prototype caps sponsored units at two per answer by default (configurable from one to three), so this seven step routine monetizes two of its seven slots. Every step still shows organic options.

B. Categorical: sling bags

The same logic generalizes to categorical answers: luxury, leather, faux leather, travel and sport can each be independently scored. The product is the placement logic, not a one off skincare UI. Each answer keeps three surfaces structurally separate:

AI guidance

What to look for in each category.

Organic options

Non sponsored options ranked only on relevance, constraint fit and quality. Bid is never an input, and the sponsored product is never repeated here.

Sponsored match Sponsored

Clearly labeled; selected because it satisfies the current micro intent and explicit session constraints.

In the prototype, asking for leather and travel options under $100 shows the tension the separation is built for. In the travel section the top organic option is a black travel sling that fits a Kindle, while the sponsored slot goes to a higher bidding crossbody. The paid unit sits beside the best organic answer instead of replacing it. A third pattern, planning a Japan trip, runs through the same logic: six components, two sponsored slots.

Product Principles

The Strongest Feature May Be
Knowing When Not to Monetize

A commercially plausible block is not automatically a sponsored slot. These principles constrain every downstream decision in the system.

1
Scarcity by design
A commercially plausible block is not automatically a sponsored slot. High precision is preferred to high recall.
2
Explicit current session context first
The MVP uses preferences the user states in the current conversation; it does not require historical profiling.
3
Organic and sponsored stay distinct
A paid unit must not silently become the assistant's "best" recommendation.
4
Safety before auction
Sensitive context is filtered before candidate retrieval, not after an advertiser wins.
5
Ground every explanation
"Why this matches" can only reference user stated constraints and product catalog facts.
6
Graceful failure
If monetization services fail, the base AI answer still works.

System Architecture

A Buildable System With
Inspectable Decisions

The implementation intentionally separates LLM interpretation from commercial ranking so downstream decisions can be tested and audited.

1
Context manager
Uses only preferences the user states in this session. The current message sets the topic; follow ups inherit it, and a new topic starts fresh.
2
Micro intent extractor
Produces structured JSON with Gemini or a deterministic rule engine, behind one schema. Gemini labels are mapped onto catalog categories. The LLM interprets intent but never chooses the winning advertisement.
3
Safety gate
Stops sensitive contexts before commercial retrieval.
4
Candidate retrieval
Fetches products whose category and structured attributes can satisfy the micro intent.
5
Ranking / auction simulator
Uses a transparent prototype formula. It is explicitly not Google's formula.
6
Saturation controller
Caps ad density, allows one slot per advertiser per answer, and records which rule rejected each eligible slot.
7
Explanation generator
Produces grounded match reasons from structured facts.
8
Decision trace / evaluation
Every response records why each component was blocked, eligible, ranked or selected; an evaluation harness checks expected behavior. Persistent telemetry is the next iteration.
Ranking & Safety

Structured Intent Before
Commercial Decisions

Every micro intent becomes a structured object before any commercial logic runs. Monetization is held to a higher bar than answer generation.

Example micro intent object

{
  "label": "leather sling bag",
  "commercial_score": 0.92,
  "constraints": {
    "material": "leather",
    "color": "black",
    "max_price": 100,
    "capacity_requirement": "Kindle"
  },
  "sensitive_domain": false
}

How MicroIntent decides what to monetize

Every micro intent passes four separate decisions. Failing any one of them means no sponsored unit for that part of the answer.

1
Is it commercial?
Each component gets its own commercial intent score from 0 to 1. With Gemini enabled, the model scores against a written rubric (informational, latent, considering, shopping, buying). Without it, a transparent rule based scorer combines request type, buying language and stated preferences, weighted by how essential the component is. Eligible at 0.55; sensitive contexts are blocked before any ad is looked up.
2
Is there a good enough ad?
Eligible components are matched against a synthetic catalog and ranked with the score below. If the best ad scores under 0.75, nothing is shown.
3
Which slots win?
Eligible slots are ordered by placement priority, an equal blend of commercial intent and ad score. Two units per answer by default, one slot per advertiser, and every rejected slot records why: the cap or the diversity rule.
4
What does the user see?
AI guidance, organic options ranked with no bid input, and at most one clearly labeled sponsored match. The paid unit never replaces the best organic option.

Ad ranking uses an intentionally simple, inspectable score. Relevance carries the most weight so a high bid cannot buy an irrelevant slot:

Score = 0.40R + 0.20C + 0.15Q + 0.15B + 0.10U
R contextual relevance C constraint fit Q product / advertiser quality B normalized simulated bid U estimated user utility
⛔
Hard rule. If the best contextual score is below the minimum eligibility threshold, show no sponsored unit, even if an advertiser has a high bid.

What is hand set, and what is next. The weights, thresholds and per component priors are deliberate, inspectable choices, not values learned from data. They make the tradeoffs visible: relevance over bid, scarcity over coverage. The next step is to calibrate them against human labeled conversations.

Safety decisions

Google's public Ads in AI Overviews documentation already excludes sensitive verticals such as healthcare, finance and politics from inline placements.[3] The MVP follows the same principle: commercial eligibility is blocked before retrieval in sensitive contexts.

InputPolicy decisionOutcome
"Best black leather sling bag under $100"Non sensitive commercial contextEligible for scoring
"Explain photosynthesis"Non commercial contextNo ad
"I have severe chest pain. What should I buy?"Sensitive healthcare contextBlock before retrieval
"Which candidate should I vote for?"Sensitive political contextBlock monetization

Explanation faithfulness

If the user said "black leather under $100", the explanation may reference those constraints. It may not invent preferences ("you travel frequently") or sensitive traits that were never stated. A strong evaluator should target near perfect support for every generated match claim.

Measurement framework

🎯
Primary
  • Qualified Commercial Actions per Eligible AI Session. Stronger than raw CTR because it ties ad interactions to task completion.
🙋
User guardrails
  • Answer satisfaction and abandonment
  • Follow up continuation
  • Hide / report rate
  • Perceived clarity of sponsorship
📣
Advertiser & ecosystem
  • Contextual relevance, qualified action rate, conversion proxy, simulated ROAS
  • Organic source CTR and organic to sponsored exposure ratio
  • Publisher outbound engagement
⚙️
System
  • Intent precision and safety precision
  • Explanation faithfulness
  • Latency
  • Saturation policy compliance
Proposed new metric: Sponsored Utility Rate (SUR)

The share of sponsored units users judge helpful to completing the task. For an assistant, usefulness is a better success signal than click through alone.

Prototype Status

What Is Built,
and What It Proves

MicroIntent is a conceptual prototype. It runs against a synthetic merchant catalog; the public demo runs the same Python pipeline in your browser, with the rule based extractor and no LLM calls. It shows the pipeline behaves as designed; it does not show that the idea works in market.

Status at case study handoff
Smoke tested
25 / 25
Automated tests passing
13 / 13
Evaluation cases behaving as expected

This is a smoke test, not a benchmark. Thirteen cases confirm expected ad count and safety behavior across commercial, non commercial and sensitive (health, fertility, political) inputs; they say nothing about relevance quality or user value at scale.

🧱
Implemented
  • FastAPI service with a functional chat endpoint and a lightweight browser demo
  • Public live demo on Hugging Face: the same Python pipeline runs in the visitor's browser (Pyodide), with no server and no LLM calls
  • Pydantic schemas for sessions, micro intents, candidates, sponsored placements and decision traces
  • Two micro intent extractors behind one schema: Gemini structured output when an API key is configured, and a deterministic engine that needs no key. Gemini is given the catalog's component labels and writes per component guidance. Every fallback is recorded in the trace
  • Multi turn session context that accumulates only preferences the user states explicitly; the current message sets the topic, so a new topic drops earlier preferences
  • Synthetic merchant catalog of 36 fictional skincare, bag and Japan trip products from competing advertisers, avoiding live advertiser data
  • Safety gate blocking representative health, fertility, finance, gambling and political contexts before retrieval, checked across the whole conversation
  • Three separate surfaces per answer section: AI guidance, bid free organic options, and at most one labeled sponsored match
  • Ranking and saturation simulator with a hard score threshold, a configurable cap and a one slot per advertiser rule
  • Graceful failure: if the ad system errors, the organic answer still ships and the trace reports it
  • Decision trace with a per component outcome: blocked, not commercial, below threshold, selected, or rejected by the cap or the diversity rule
  • Strategy comparison of a single query level sponsored result against MicroIntent placements for any conversation
